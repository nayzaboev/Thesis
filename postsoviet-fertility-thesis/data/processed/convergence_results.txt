  Reading: a negative slope means the countries in that grouping grew more

  within a bloc while the gap BETWEEN blocs widens - these are separate facts.

========================================================================
(B) BETA-CONVERGENCE — catch-up across the 14 countries
========================================================================
Specification: (1/21) * ln(TFR_end / TFR_start) = alpha + beta * ln(TFR_start)
Endpoints are 3-year averages: start = mean(2000-2002), end = mean(2021-2023).
n = 14 countries. HC3 standard errors with small-sample t inference.

  UNCONDITIONAL beta-convergence:
    beta = +0.0013  (HC3 SE 0.0116, p = 0.911)
    95% CI [-0.0240, +0.0267]     R2 = 0.004
    => Positive: no evidence of catch-up. Initially higher-fertility
       countries did NOT decline faster; if anything the opposite.

  CONDITIONAL beta-convergence (adds the Central Asia dummy):
    ln_tfr_start    : -0.0201  (HC3 SE 0.0267, p = 0.467)
    ca              : +0.0176  (HC3 SE 0.0187, p = 0.368)
    R2 = 0.270
    Reading: the CA dummy asks whether Central Asian countries followed a
    different trajectory once their 2000 starting level is accounted for.
    A positive CA coefficient means Central Asia declined less (or rose
    more) than its starting level alone would predict.

  CAVEAT (Galton's fallacy, Quah 1993): beta-convergence is necessary but
  NOT sufficient for sigma-convergence, and a negative beta can arise from
  regression to the mean if initial TFR is measured with error. Read
  sections (A) and (B) together, and treat both as descriptive summaries
  at n = 14.

========================================================================
(E) PROJECTION-SENSITIVITY CHECK (descriptive only)
========================================================================
master_tfr.csv carries 'estimate_method', distinguishing WPP retrospective
'Interpolation' from forward-looking 'Projection' values (20 of 336 rows,
concentrated in 2020-2023 and in every country's 2023 observation; see
01_clean_data.py). The (B2) post-2017 CV slope and the (B) beta-convergence
coefficient above both partly rest on these projected endpoints. This block
re-computes those two headline descriptive results under three samples:
  (a) full 2000-2023 (baseline, as reported above)
  (b) data through 2019 only
  (c) excluding rows where estimate_method == 'Projection'
This is NOT a significance test: no p-values or significance claims are made
here, the sub-samples are smaller than the already-small n=14/T=7 used above,
and sample (c) is a ragged panel (a country contributes a year only if its
own value that year is Interpolation, not Projection).

  Sample                            CV slope 2000-16  CV slope post-16      beta  n (beta)  end window used
  (a) Full 2000-2023 (baseline)             -0.00715          +0.01954   +0.0013        14  2021-2023 (post-16: 2017-2023)
  (b) Through 2019 only                     -0.00715          +0.02365   -0.0085        14  2017-2019 (post-16: 2017-2019)
  (c) Excl. Projection rows                 -0.00715          -0.01934   +0.0074        11  2021-2023 (post-16: 2017-2023)

  Reading: compare the sign and rough magnitude of the post-2016 CV slope and
  of beta across rows (a)-(c). If they hold the same sign once projected years
  are removed or excluded (b, c), the post-2017 divergence and catch-up pattern
  reported above are not simply an artifact of WPP projections. Sample (c) has
  a smaller, ragged n for beta because countries whose most recent years are
  themselves projections (e.g. Belarus, Tajikistan) drop out of its end window.
  All entries in this block are descriptive point estimates; treat as a
  sensitivity check, not a formal robustness test.
