Trend test: regress CV on a linear year term.
Newey-West SEs (4 lags) because CV series are strongly serially correlated.
A negative, significant slope indicates sigma-convergence.

  All 14 countries                : slope/yr = -0.00070  p = 0.758   (convergence, not significant)
  Bloc: Central Asia              : slope/yr = -0.00751  p = 0.000   (convergence, significant)
  Bloc: Rest of post-Soviet       : slope/yr = +0.00163  p = 0.025   (divergence, significant)
  Sub-group: Baltic               : slope/yr = -0.00097  p = 0.359   (convergence, not significant)
  Sub-group: Caucasus             : slope/yr = +0.00007  p = 0.972   (divergence, not significant)
  Sub-group: Central Asia         : slope/yr = -0.00751  p = 0.000   (convergence, significant)
  Sub-group: Eastern European     : slope/yr = +0.00435  p = 0.050   (divergence, significant)

  Reading: a negative slope means the countries in that grouping grew more
  alike; a positive slope means they diverged. Note that dispersion can fall
  within a bloc while the gap BETWEEN blocs widens - these are separate facts.

========================================================================
(B) BETA-CONVERGENCE — catch-up across the 14 countries
========================================================================
Specification: (1/23) * ln(TFR_2023 / TFR_2000) = alpha + beta * ln(TFR_2000)
n = 14 countries. HC3 standard errors (small sample).

  UNCONDITIONAL beta-convergence:
    beta = +0.0030  (HC3 SE 0.0107, p = 0.780)
    95% CI [-0.0179, +0.0239]     R2 = 0.019
    => Positive: no evidence of catch-up. Initially higher-fertility
       countries did NOT decline faster; if anything the opposite.

  CONDITIONAL beta-convergence (adds the Central Asia dummy):
    ln_tfr_start    : -0.0175  (HC3 SE 0.0212, p = 0.409)
    ca              : +0.0171  (HC3 SE 0.0148, p = 0.247)
    R2 = 0.294
    Reading: the CA dummy asks whether Central Asian countries followed a
    different trajectory once their 2000 starting level is accounted for.
    A positive CA coefficient means Central Asia declined less (or rose
    more) than its starting level alone would predict.

  CAVEAT (Galton's fallacy, Quah 1993): beta-convergence is necessary but
  NOT sufficient for sigma-convergence, and a negative beta can arise from
  regression to the mean if initial TFR is measured with error. Read
  sections (A) and (B) together, and treat both as descriptive summaries
  at n = 14.

  widening after 2017 (documented in Layer A, script 15).
