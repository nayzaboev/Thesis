CROSS-SECTION ANALYSIS — cultural correlates of the CA fertility premium (n=14)

====================================================================
Cross-section n=14 — cultural correlates of the fertility premium
====================================================================

Upfront caveat: corr(CA dummy, Muslim share) = +0.827.
Cultural variables largely re-describe the CA / non-CA split;
they cannot uniquely identify religion vs ethnicity vs family norms.

====================================================================
(C) Interpretation
====================================================================
  Muslim share alone: R2 = 0.766
  Muslim + SMAM together: R2 = 0.776
  Bivariate TFR-schooling correlation: r = -0.581 — weak, and consistent
  with the Soviet-era compression of female education across the region.

The cross-section is consistent with the interpretation that religious-cultural
factors and nuptiality regimes are associated with the fertility gap that survives
economic controls in Layer A. It cannot identify these factors causally: n=14 is
too small, cultural variables are collinear with Central Asia status, and cross-
sectional averages cannot distinguish religion from ethnicity, family norms, or
historical legacy. Azerbaijan (95% Muslim, mean TFR 1.88) also shows that Muslim
share alone does not determine fertility — the story involves religion INTERACTING
with nuptiality patterns and demographic history, not religion by itself.
