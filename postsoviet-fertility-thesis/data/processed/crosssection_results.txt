====================================================================
(C) Inseparability test: can Muslim share be distinguished from CA status?
====================================================================

  Joint model on mean TFR (HC3 standard errors):
    CA:           +0.953  (SE 1.645, p=0.562)
    Muslim share: +0.0047  (SE 0.0185, p=0.799)

  Joint model on country residuals (HC3 standard errors):
    CA:           +0.620  (SE 0.224, p=0.006)
    Muslim share: -0.0023  (SE 0.0023, p=0.328)

  FINDING: Muslim share adds NO explanatory power once CA status is included.
  The two variables are empirically inseparable at n=14 (r=0.83).
  This does NOT mean religion is irrelevant — it means this dataset cannot
  distinguish Central Asian regional identity from Muslim population share.
  The cultural interpretation must therefore rest on the LITERATURE
  (Spoorenberg, Kumo & Perugini, Dommaraju & Agadjanian), not on the
  cross-sectional regression. The regression documents the inseparability;
  the literature carries the interpretation.

====================================================================
(D) Summary interpretation
====================================================================
  Muslim share alone: R2 = 0.766
  Muslim + SMAM: R2 = 0.776
  Bivariate TFR-schooling: r = -0.581

  The cross-section documents strong bivariate associations between TFR
  and Muslim share / SMAM, and a moderate negative association with female
  schooling (r = -0.58) that disappears in the residuals (r = +0.19),
  suggesting it reflects the same regional split rather than an education effect.
  However, the inseparability test (C) shows that Muslim share cannot be
  distinguished from Central Asian regional identity in this sample.
  Azerbaijan (95% Muslim, TFR 1.88) further demonstrates that Muslim
  share alone does not determine fertility.

  The defensible conclusion: the cross-section is CONSISTENT with the
  literature's emphasis on religious-cultural and nuptiality regimes,
  but it cannot IDENTIFY these factors separately from regional identity.
  Causal attribution is not possible at n=14 with these data.

====================================================================
(E) Temporal alignment: 2018-2022 mean TFR vs cultural variables
====================================================================
Cultural variables are measured ~2020. If full-period mean TFR (2000-2023)
gives different correlations than a 2018-2022 window aligned with the
cultural measurement dates, the results may be driven by temporal mismatch.

  Correlations: full period (2000-2023) vs aligned window (2018-2022):
    Muslim share (%)                    : r(full)=+0.875  |  r(2018-22)=+0.852  |  Δ=-0.024
    Female SMAM (years)                 : r(full)=-0.568  |  r(2018-22)=-0.541  |  Δ=+0.027
    Female mean schooling (years)       : r(full)=-0.581  |  r(2018-22)=-0.493  |  Δ=+0.088

  Interpretation: if the correlations are similar, temporal mismatch
  between cultural variables (~2020) and TFR (2000-2023) is not a concern.
  Large divergences would suggest the cross-section is period-sensitive.
