  Interpretation: the Mundlak hybrid separates long-run cross-country
  associations (between) from short-run within-country changes (within).
  The CA premium is essentially unchanged from pooled M2, confirming it
  is not an artifact of mixing between/within variation.
  The formal Mundlak RE-vs-FE test (H0: between = within coefficients)
  is reported in 17_diagnostics.py. In this sample it FAILS to reject H0
  (F(4,13)=1.34, p=0.31), so there is no statistical evidence that RE is
  inconsistent; the hybrid/FE specification is retained on substantive
  grounds, and the CA coefficient is numerically similar to pooled M2.

  CA gap in 2008-2016: +0.888  (base +0.795 + Δ +0.093, interaction p=0.551)
  CA gap in 2017-2023: +1.174  (base +0.795 + Δ +0.379, interaction p=0.101)

  R2: 0.918

  95% confidence intervals on the period interactions:
    Delta 2008-2016: +0.093  CI [-0.214, +0.401]  p=0.551
    Delta 2017-2023: +0.379  CI [-0.074, +0.831]  p=0.101

  HONEST READING: the point estimate of the gap rises across periods
  (+0.79 -> +0.89 -> +1.17), and the raw descriptive gap rises too
  (1.23 -> 1.24 -> 1.50). HOWEVER, neither period interaction is
  statistically significant at conventional levels, and the 95% CI for
  the 2017-2023 term includes zero. With 14 clusters the test is
  underpowered. The correct claim is therefore: 'the gap did not narrow,
  and the point estimates suggest widening after 2017, but the widening
  cannot be statistically distinguished from no change in this sample.'
  Do NOT write that the gap 'significantly widened'.
