\subsection{Projection sensitivity check}
\label{app:projection-check}

Table~\ref{tab:proj-check} recomputes the post-2016 dispersion slope and the $\beta$-convergence coefficient on two balanced sub-samples, each holding a fixed country set. Sample A uses all fourteen countries over 2000--2019, a window containing no projected values. Sample B uses the ten countries with a non-projected observation in every year from 2017 to 2022 (Azerbaijan, Estonia, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Latvia, Lithuania, Moldova, Russia, Uzbekistan), with that set held fixed across all years compared.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Projection sensitivity: dispersion slopes and $\beta$-convergence}
\label{tab:proj-check}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Sample & CV slope 2000--16 & CV slope post-2016 & $\beta$ & $n$ \\
\midrule
Full (baseline) & $-0.0072$ & $+0.0195$ & $+0.0013$ & 14 \\
Sample A (2000--19) & $-0.0072$ & $+0.0237$ & $-0.0085$ & 14 \\
Sample B (interp.\ only) & $-0.0033$ & $+0.0261$ & $+0.0052$ & 10 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}\footnotesize
\vspace{4pt}
\emph{Note:} The post-2016 dispersion slope is positive in all three samples. Sample B covers six annual points (2017--2022) and rises monotonically. The $\beta$ sign is not stable, but in all three samples it is small, imprecise and consistent with no detectable catch-up. No p-values are attached; the sub-samples are too small for reliable inference.
\end{minipage}
\end{table}

\subsection{Variance inflation factors}
\label{app:vif}

Table~\ref{tab:vif} reports variance inflation factors for the four lagged controls, computed three ways to locate the source of collinearity.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Variance inflation factors: pooled, between and within components}
\label{tab:vif}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrl}
\toprule
Variable & Pooled & Between & Within & Flag \\
\midrule
log GDP p.c.\ PPP (lag) & 5.60 & 14.34 & 3.86 & Between: serious \\
Urban population \% (lag) & 4.87 & 13.34 & 1.55 & Between: serious \\
Remittances \% GDP (lag) & 4.13 & 17.05 & 1.39 & Between: serious \\
Under-5 mortality (lag) & 3.94 & 5.51 & 4.40 & Between: concerning \\
Central Asia indicator & --- & 3.06 & --- & OK \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}\footnotesize
\vspace{4pt}
\emph{Note:} Conventional thresholds: VIF $> 5$ concerning, $> 10$ serious. Between-country VIFs for GDP, urbanisation and remittances are all above 10, indicating severe collinearity among country means. Within-country VIFs are all below 5. The Central Asia indicator has a moderate between VIF of 3.06, and its coefficient is stable across pooled and hybrid specifications. The between-country control coefficients in the Mundlak model are individually uninterpretable and are not tabulated in the body.
\end{minipage}
\end{table}
