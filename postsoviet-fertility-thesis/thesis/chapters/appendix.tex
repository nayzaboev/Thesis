\subsection{Projection sensitivity check}
\label{app:projection-check}

Table~\ref{tab:proj-check} recomputes the post-2016 dispersion slope and the $\beta$-convergence coefficient on two balanced sub-samples, each holding a fixed country set. Sample A uses all fourteen countries over 2000--2019, a window containing no projected values. Sample B uses the ten countries with a non-projected observation in every year from 2017 to 2022 (Azerbaijan, Estonia, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Latvia, Lithuania, Moldova, Russia, Uzbekistan), with that set held fixed across all years compared.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Projection sensitivity: dispersion slopes and $\beta$-convergence}
\label{tab:proj-check}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
Sample & CV slope 2000--16 & CV slope post-2016 & $\beta$ & $n$ \\
\midrule
Full (baseline) & $-0.0072$ & $+0.0195$ & $+0.0013$ & 14 \\
Sample A (2000--19) & $-0.0072$ & $+0.0237$ & $-0.0085$ & 14 \\
Sample B (interp.\ only) & $-0.0033$ & $+0.0261$ & $+0.0052$ & 10 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}\footnotesize
\vspace{4pt}
\emph{Note:} The post-2016 dispersion slope is positive in all three samples. Sample B covers six annual points (2017--2022) and rises monotonically. The $\beta$ sign is not stable, but in all three samples it is small, imprecise and consistent with no detectable catch-up. No p-values are attached; the sub-samples are too small for reliable inference.
\end{minipage}
\end{table}

\subsection{Exclusion and war-period robustness}
\label{app:exclusions}

Table~\ref{tab:exclusions} reports the targeted exclusions and the period-wide war exclusions discussed in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}. The upper panel drops specific country-episodes from the controlled specification. The lower panel removes the war years for all fourteen countries simultaneously, since the 2022 invasion of Ukraine was a regional rather than a national shock, and reports all three between-country specifications.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering

\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}

\midrule
\multicolumn{5}{l}{\emph{Panel A: targeted exclusions (M2)}} \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\begin{minipage}{\textwidth}\footnotesize
\vspace{4pt}
\emph{Note:} Exact $p$-values enumerate all $2^G$ Rademacher sign assignments, where $G$ is the number of countries retained: 14 in the baseline, the year-restricted exclusions and the period-wide exclusions, and 13 where an entire country is removed. Panel B removes the stated years for every country, leaving the primary hybrid estimate at $+0.898$ under the milder cut against $+0.964$ in the full sample; the M1 and M2 estimates clear five per cent under both cuts, but the M2h estimate does not. The corresponding check on the descriptive dispersion trends appears in Section~\ref{sec:desc-robust}: Central Asian internal convergence is unaffected, while the divergence among the other ten countries weakens from $+0.0016$ to $+0.0007$ per year and falls below conventional significance ($p=0.088$). Exact $p$-values follow the tie convention described under Table~\ref{tab:loo-m2}.
\end{minipage}
